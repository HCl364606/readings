It often seems as though capitalist societies exist in a state of perpetual crisis regarding the issue of work. Consider the United States, long home to the world’s largest economy and where, in 2008, a financial crisis originating in the housing sector triggered a severe and extended economic slow-down. As the “Great Recession” entrenched itself, indebted consumers cut back on spending while businesses and governments scaled back hiring and laid off employees. By 2011 the country as a whole was 12 million jobs short of the number needed to provide employment for all eligible adults – not to mention the additional 25 million people working part-time but desiring full-time work.
在就业问题上，资本主义社会似乎总是处于一种永无休止的危机状态。以美国为例，这个长期以来拥有世界最大经济体的国家，在2008年，一场源于房地产行业的金融危机引发了严重的、持续的经济衰退。随着“大衰退”的持续，负债累累的消费者削减开支，企业和政府也缩减了招聘规模并裁员。到2011年，全美就业岗位比满足所有符合条件的成年人就业所需的岗位数量少了1200万个——更不用说还有2500万人从事兼职工作，却渴望全职工作。

The nation’s mood was dark, to say the least. Night after night, news programs featured heartbreaking stories of hopeless job-seekers queuing up at employment agencies. Politicians from both sides of the aisle hammered Democratic President Barack Obama for his lack of a comprehensive “jobs plan” to address an unemployment rate hovering around 10 percent. While many offered different remedies for what ailed the economy, the underlying diagnosis was widely agreed upon: industrious Americans were desperately seeking work, but the jobs were simply not there.
整个国家的气氛十分低迷。夜复一夜，新闻节目都在报道令人心碎的求职者在就业中心排长队的悲惨故事。两党政客都猛烈抨击民主党总统奥巴马，指责他缺乏全面的“就业计划”来应对徘徊在10%左右的失业率。尽管许多人提出了不同的经济解决方案，但问题的根本原因却得到了广泛的认同：勤劳的美国人迫切地想要工作，但工作机会却寥寥无几。

In the midst of such a crisis, it was only too easy to forget that scarcely a decade before – in fact, during the previous Democratic presidential administration – the United States faced the opposite problem. During the 1990s, driven by a dot-com investment boom, stock markets increased in value, consumers went on a spending binge, and firms went on a hiring spree. With the unemployment rate dropping under 4 percent and a surplus of job openings, the American economy was plagued not by a lack of jobs but by a lack of workers! Especially for employers in the low-wage sectors of the economy (such as food service and home health care), it was difficult to attract staff at the prevailing minimum wage. The dominant discourse on employment shifted as well, and it soon became bipartisan “common sense” that the problem haunting America was an eroding work ethic.
在这样一场危机之中，人们很容易忘记，仅仅十年前——实际上是在上一届民主党总统执政期间——美国面临的问题恰恰相反。上世纪90年代，在互联网泡沫的推动下，股市飙升，消费者疯狂消费，企业也大举招聘。失业率降至4%以下，职位空缺大量涌现，美国经济面临的困境并非就业岗位短缺，而是劳动力短缺！尤其对于低薪行业（例如餐饮服务和家庭护理）的雇主而言，以当时的最低工资标准招到员工更是难上加难。关于就业的主流观点也随之转变，很快，两党都普遍认为，困扰美国的根本问题是职业道德的沦丧。

The system of entitlements that had been put in place over the preceding decades to protect vulnerable Americans, this thinking went, had backfired. An overly generous “welfare state” had created a coddled and indolent class of job-shirkers (Collins and Mayer 2010). In truth, even at its peak, the welfare system in the United States was less generous than that of other rich industrialized nations (Esping-Andersen 1990; Steensland 2007). Nonetheless, President Clinton, in 1996, signed the Personal Responsibility and Work Opportunity Act, a piece of legislation intended to “end welfare as we know it” (Clinton 2006). It forced recipients of public assistance to sing for their proverbial suppers, no matter how menial the employment they acquired.
这种观点认为，过去几十年为保护弱势美国民众而建立的福利制度适得其反。过于慷慨的“福利国家”造就了一批娇生惯养、游手好闲的懒汉阶层（Collins and Mayer 2010）。事实上，即使在其鼎盛时期，美国的福利制度也远不如其他富裕的工业化国家慷慨（Esping-Andersen 1990; Steensland 2007）。尽管如此，克林顿总统还是在 1996 年签署了《个人责任与工作机会法案》，这项旨在“终结我们所知的福利制度”的法案（Clinton 2006）。它迫使公共援助的领取者必须拼命工作才能获得报酬，无论他们从事的工作多么卑微。

A seemingly permanent “crisis of work” is not unique to the United States. For some time now, Europe has been plagued by high unemployment and economic stagnation, a situation many attribute to inflexible labor markets. Burdensome state regulations and powerful labor unions, critics argue, have made it difficult for firms to hire and fire workers in response to fast-shifting business conditions.
看似永无止境的“就业危机”并非美国独有。一段时间以来，欧洲一直饱受高失业率和经济停滞的困扰，许多人将此归咎于劳动力市场缺乏灵活性。批评人士认为，繁琐的州级法规和强大的工会使得企业难以根据快速变化的商业环境灵活地雇佣和解雇员工。

Meanwhile, in China, decades of economic reform have liberalized the economy and produced staggering economic growth. In fact, in early 2011, China surpassed Japan to become the world’s second largest economy. Accompanying this rise in output, however, has been a surge in protests and strikes by workers in the country’s burgeoning factory system (Lee 2007). As they produce more and more goods for the world’s (and especially America’s) consumers, Chinese workers appear to be increasingly unsatisfied with their poor working conditions and lack of socio-economic mobility. Similar stories of labor discontent can be found in the world’s other emerging economies, such as Brazil, South Korea, South Africa, and India (Silver 2003).
与此同时，在中国，数十年的经济改革使经济自由化，并带来了惊人的经济增长。事实上，2011 年初，中国超越日本，成为世界第二大经济体。然而，伴随产出增长而来的，是中国蓬勃发展的工厂体系中工人抗议和罢工的激增（Lee 2007）。随着他们为世界（尤其是美国）消费者生产越来越多的商品，中国工人似乎越来越不满于恶劣的工作条件和缺乏社会经济流动性。类似的劳工不满情绪在其他新兴经济体，如巴西、韩国、南非和印度，也屡见不鲜（Silver 2003）。

The purpose of this book is to mobilize the conceptual tools of economic sociology to understand the place and fate of work under global capitalism. Economic sociology is ideally suited to this task. As an academic field it endeavors to put capitalism in world-historical perspective, as but one among many potential ways to organize economic activity. How, it asks, did private property and labor markets come to constitute the pillars of the modern global order? Why is the contemporary world converging upon an extreme version of capitalism (the free-market, or neoliberal, version most closely associated with the United States)? Is it folly to even imagine a post-capitalist future?
本书旨在运用经济社会学的概念工具，理解全球资本主义下劳动者的地位和命运。经济社会学非常适合这项任务。作为一个学术领域，它致力于将资本主义置于世界历史的视角下进行考察，将其视为众多经济活动组织方式之一。本书提出的问题是：私有财产和劳动力市场是如何成为现代全球秩序的支柱的？为什么当今世界正趋向于一种极端的资本主义模式（即与美国联系最为紧密的自由市场或新自由主义模式）？设想一个后资本主义的未来是否愚蠢至极？

To address such questions, economic sociologists have assembled a range of theories, ideas, and methods. At the center of and unifying this assemblage is one key insight: that markets are social structures (Martin 2009). They come into being not as the expression of some natural law, but as strategic action projects that achieve success at particular historical moments (Fligstein and McAdam 2012; Hall and Soskice 2001). Markets of labor are no exception. To transform the basic human capacity to engage in meaningful work into a commodity requires persuasion, coercion, and ongoing intervention. To challenge the resulting fiction requires strategic action as well. As the paradigm of regulated capitalism (in which labor is afforded basic protections from the constant pressure of the market) gives way to unfettered market fundamentalism, it becomes ever more important to understand how these conflicts over labor’s status as a commodity play out.
为了解答这些问题，经济社会学家汇集了一系列理论、思想和方法。贯穿并统一这些理论、思想和方法的关键洞见是：市场是社会结构（Martin 2009）。它们的出现并非源于某种自然法则，而是作为战略行动项目在特定历史时期取得成功的结果（Fligstein and McAdam 2012; Hall and Soskice 2001）。劳动力市场也不例外。将人类从事有意义工作的基本能力转化为商品，需要说服、强制和持续干预。挑战由此产生的虚构也需要战略行动。随着受监管的资本主义范式（在该范式中，劳动者受到基本保护，免受市场持续压力的影响）让位于不受约束的市场原教旨主义，理解围绕劳动商品地位的冲突如何展开变得愈发重要。

Of Metaphors and Markets
论隐喻与市场

People have a natural tendency to think metaphorically. When confronted with something new and puzzling, we make sense of it by comparing it to something that is already available in our vocabulary. It is thus not surprising that so many observers look at the crises of work that continue to plague us and attempt to make sense of them through organic or mechanical metaphors. That is, they compare the society under consideration to a sick person or a malfunctioning machine. If the unemployment rate in France suddenly spikes, we will surely hear a diagnosis of “Eurosclerosis.” If the United States’ inflation rate is too high, it could well be the case that the country’s “jobs machine” is “overheating.” And just as a physician prescribes a cure or a mechanic proposes a fix, the observer feels compelled to propose a line of action (typically a new policy or the modification of an existing one) that will bring the economy back to a healthy condition.
人们天生倾向于用比喻思考。当面对新鲜且令人困惑的事物时，我们会通过将其与词汇库中已有的事物进行比较来理解它。因此，许多观察者审视持续困扰我们的就业危机，并试图用有机或机械的比喻来解释它们，也就不足为奇了。也就是说，他们将所研究的社会比作病人或故障的机器。如果法国的失业率突然飙升，我们肯定会听到“经济硬化症”的诊断。如果美国的通货膨胀率过高，很可能是该国的“就业机器”“过热”。正如医生开药方或修理工提出修理方案一样，观察者也感到有必要提出一套行动方案（通常是新政策或对现有政策的修改），以使经济恢复健康状态。

But what exactly would such a condition look like? How often, and for how long, do actually existing capitalist economies appear to exist in such a state? Let’s return to the example of the United States. Many have argued that it most closely approximates a pure, or, in the words of the sociologist Max Weber, “ideal type” capitalist economy. Among all the countries in the world today, that is, the United States most fully embraces the principles of private property and market exchange. Not only the United States’ continued leadership in the world economy, but the fact that so many countries have attempted to imitate its model of economic regulation over the past several decades, suggests that the US system of capitalism, despite its recent weaknesses, will continue to be a dominant paradigm for the foreseeable future. Nonetheless, even a cursory glance confirms that the US economy is notable precisely for its continual failure to meet its own standards of a “healthy” work system (Mishel et al., 2009).
但这种状态究竟会是什么样子？现实中的资本主义经济体究竟多久、又会以何种频率处于这种状态？让我们回到美国的例子。许多人认为，美国最接近纯粹的，或者用社会学家马克斯·韦伯的话来说，最接近“理想型”资本主义经济体。也就是说，在当今世界所有国家中，美国最充分地奉行私有财产和市场交换的原则。美国不仅持续引领世界经济，而且在过去几十年里，许多国家都试图效仿其经济监管模式，这表明，尽管美国资本主义体系近期出现了一些弱点，但在可预见的未来，它仍将是一种主导范式。然而，即使是粗略地观察一下也能发现，美国经济的显著特点恰恰在于它始终未能达到自身设定的“健康”工作体系的标准（Mishel et al., 2009）。

Consider a handful of basic indicators, starting with an unemployment rate that recently peaked at 10 percent. Economists and policy-makers speak of a salubrious state of “full employment.” Somewhat counterintuitively, this does not mean a situation in which every single person who seeks a job will be able to find one, but rather one in which 95 percent of job-seekers can and do. In other words, an unemployment rate of 5 percent is acknowledged to be ideal for a well-functioning capitalist economy. However, if we look at the data for the past two decades (figure 1.1), we see that the actual unemployment rate hits this number only sporadically, on its way up or down. It’s as if a hospital patient’s temperature could not be maintained at a steady 98.6 degrees Fahrenheit, but swung back and forth between a bad fever and awful chills!
让我们来看几个基本指标，首先是最近达到10%峰值的失业率。经济学家和政策制定者常说“充分就业”是一种健康的经济状态。但与直觉有些出乎意料的是，这并非指每个求职者都能找到工作，而是指95%的求职者能够找到工作。换句话说，5%的失业率被认为是运转良好的资本主义经济的理想水平。然而，如果我们查看过去二十年的数据（图1.1），就会发现实际失业率只是偶尔达到这个数字，而且一直在上升或下降。这就像医院病人的体温无法稳定维持在华氏98.6度，而是在高烧和寒战之间来回波动！

Both of these thinkers eschewed metaphors depicting capitalism as an organism existing complacently in a state of harmonious equilibrium with its environment. Rather, they conceptualized capitalism as a dynamic, contentious, and continuously evolving social structure. For the nineteenth-century German philosopher Marx, this was a revolutionary system that generated immense wealth and unleashed powerful new methods of production. As a byproduct, however, capitalism generated repeated booms and busts, periods of economic expansion and contraction. Eventually, Marx argued, humanity would tire of weathering these repeated crises and, led by an alienated but mobilized working class, institute a post-capitalist, or communist, society characterized by collective ownership of the means of production, rational planning, and an equitable distribution of wealth.
这两位思想家都摒弃了将资本主义描绘成一个安于现状、与环境和谐共存的有机体的比喻。相反，他们将资本主义概念化为一个动态的、充满争议的、不断演变的社会结构。对于十九世纪德国哲学家马克思而言，这是一个革命性的制度，它创造了巨额财富，并催生了强大的新型生产方式。然而，资本主义的副产品是反复出现的繁荣与萧条，经济扩张与收缩的周期性交替。马克思认为，最终，人类会厌倦这些反复出现的危机，并在异化但动员起来的工人阶级的领导下，建立一个后资本主义或共产主义社会，其特征是生产资料集体所有制、理性计划和财富的公平分配。

Friges Schumpeter, an Austrian economist of the early twentieth century, concurred with Marx’s assessment that capitalism possessed the unique ability to repeatedly revolutionize accepted ways of making and doing things. As the forces of supply and demand ran their course, they would select for those market actors most suited for survival. He labeled this process, in which existing businesses were eviscerated and new ones brought into being, one of creative destruction (Schumpeter 1994 [1942]). And though Schumpeter himself believed that capitalism could not survive these eternally recurring purges, other luminaries of economic thought (e.g., Friedman 2002 [1962]; Hayek 1996 [1948]) argued the converse: although the business cycle could be painful, especially for those industries and trades that became outmoded and were displaced, on the whole a system of private ownership and market competition was the only way that society could evolve and improve. The communist system that Marx proposed, in contrast, would ultimately stifle humanity’s entrepreneurial spirit.
二十世纪初的奥地利经济学家弗里格斯·熊彼特赞同马克思的观点，即资本主义拥有不断革新既有生产和生产方式的独特能力。随着供求关系的运行，市场参与者会选择那些最能生存下来的企业。他将这一过程——现有企业被淘汰，新企业诞生——称为“创造性破坏”（熊彼特，1994 [1942]）。尽管熊彼特本人认为资本主义无法在这种永无止境的淘汰中生存下来，但其他一些经济学界的杰出人物（例如弗里德曼，2002 [1962]；哈耶克，1996 [1948]）却持相反观点：尽管经济周期可能带来痛苦，尤其是对于那些过时且被淘汰的行业和贸易而言，但总体而言，私有制和市场竞争体系是社会发展和进步的唯一途径。与此相反，马克思提出的共产主义制度最终会扼杀人类的创业精神。

In many ways, the story of the twentieth century was one of these contrasting visions. Following the Second World War, there arose a new global order centered upon a conflict between two economic systems: that of the United States – champion of free-market capitalism – and that of the Soviet Union—whose leaders embraced, at least in principle, the central tenets of Marxism. At
在许多方面，二十世纪的历史正是这种对比鲜明的愿景的体现。二战后，一种新的全球秩序兴起，其核心是两种经济体系之间的冲突：一种是美国的经济体系——自由市场资本主义的拥护者；另一种是苏联的经济体系——其领导人至少在原则上接受了马克思主义的核心原则。

no point did these conflicting visions erupt into direct military confrontation between the two sides. Rather, there played out a Cold War in which each nation sought to expand its political influence throughout key geographical areas and to exercise cultural hegemony in the emergent “world society.” In addition, and perhaps most importantly, the United States and the Soviet Union engaged in frantic contests to design innovative and efficient methods of production. The most high profile of these took place in the fields of aeronautics (the “space race”) and weaponry (the “arms race”). But across all sectors of their economies, the two nations implemented divergent methods of organizing work and workers: the one based on central planning and state ownership, the other on market forces and private property (Burawoy and Lukács 1985).
这些相互冲突的愿景并未演变为双方之间的直接军事对抗。相反，两国展开了一场冷战，各自力图在关键地理区域扩大政治影响力，并在新兴的“世界社会”中行使文化霸权。此外，或许更为重要的是，美苏两国还展开了激烈的竞赛，致力于研发创新高效的生产方式。其中最引人注目的当属航空领域（“太空竞赛”）和武器领域（“军备竞赛”）。但在经济的各个领域，两国都实施了截然不同的工作和劳动组织方式：一个基于中央计划和国有制，另一个则基于市场力量和私有制（Burawoy and Lukács 1985）。

In 1991 the Soviet Union collapsed and the Cold War ended. It is debatable the extent to which the Soviet Union’s demise was attributable to inherent failings in a socialist system of production. In some ways it was remarkable that Russia, a rural and feudal society on the eve of its 1917 revolution, was able to transform itself into a global industrial powerhouse over the course of the twentieth century. Regardless, the collapse of the Soviet Union signaled the triumph of Schumpterian-style capitalism. Throughout the 1980s and 1990s, not only the former Soviet states but countries worldwide reformed their economic systems in line with the principle that the free market (and not central planning by state bureaucrats, traditional authorities, or an enlightened proletariat) should guide the production, distribution, and consumption of valued goods, services, and resources (Baccaro and Howell 2011).
1991 年，苏联解体，冷战结束。苏联解体在多大程度上归因于社会主义生产体系的固有缺陷，这一点尚存争议。从某种程度上来说，俄罗斯——一个在 1917 年革命前夕仍处于农村和封建状态的社会——能够在 20 世纪转型成为全球工业强国，这本身就令人瞩目。无论如何，苏联解体标​​志着熊彼特式资本主义的胜利。在整个 20 世纪 80 年代和 90 年代，不仅是前苏联加盟共和国，世界各国也纷纷按照自由市场（而非国家官僚、传统权威或开明无产阶级的中央计划）指导有价值商品、服务和资源的生产、分配和消费的原则，对其经济体系进行了改革（Baccaro 和 Howell，2011）。

In regard to the issue of work, the implications of this neoliberal revolution were clear. To increase productivity, maximize efficiency, and improve competitiveness, policy-makers must treat human labor like any other resource: that is, as a commodity to be bought and sold on the open market. British Prime Minister Margaret Thatcher, who, along with US President Ronald Reagan, spearheaded this neoliberal paradigm shift by launching attacks on labor unions and other institutions intended to protect workers from the vagaries of the market, famously stated the new common sense thus: from this day forward, “there is no alternative” to free-market capitalism (Harvey 2007).
就劳动问题而言，这场新自由主义革命的影响显而易见。为了提高生产力、最大化效率并增强竞争力，政策制定者必须将人力劳动视为与其他资源一样的商品，即在公开市场上买卖。英国首相玛格丽特·撒切尔与美国总统罗纳德·里根一道，通过攻击工会和其他旨在保护工人免受市场波动影响的机构，引领了这场新自由主义范式的转变。她曾发表过一句名言，阐述了新的常识：从今天起，“除了自由市场资本主义，别无选择”（Harvey 2007）。

But was this final chapter penned too soon? Even prior to the recession that hit in 2008, “crises of work” had not disappeared. If anything, they arose again and again, with what seemed like an ever-increasing frequency. In addition to the issues mentioned above (of unemployment, welfare reform, and stagnant wages), work remains implicated in a good many of the social problems confronting our world today: increasing inequality, illegal immigration, illicit discrimination, and on and on (Sassen 1998). In the words of economist Meghnad Desai (2004), it may be that we are witnessing “Marx’s revenge.” That is, despite the claims of free-market advocates, a system that treats human labor as a simple commodity is destined for instability. Nonetheless, with the dramatic end of Soviet socialism along with communist China’s continued drift toward a market system, there do seem to be few alternatives at hand.
但这最后一章是否写得太早了？即便在 2008 年经济衰退之前，“工作危机”也并未消失。相反，它们似乎愈演愈烈，反复出现。除了上文提到的失业、福利改革和工资停滞等问题之外，工作仍然与当今世界面临的诸多社会问题息息相关：日益加剧的不平等、非法移民、非法歧视等等（Sassen 1998）。正如经济学家梅格纳德·德赛（Meghnad Desai，2004）所言，我们或许正在目睹“马克思的复仇”。也就是说，尽管自由市场倡导者声称一切皆有可能，但将人类劳动视为简单商品的制度注定会走向不稳定。然而，随着苏联社会主义的终结以及共产主义中国持续向市场经济体制转型，似乎已别无选择。

Rather than a resurrection of Marx from the grave, it seems more likely that our era will remain one of “zombie economics” (Quiggin 2010) in which empirically discredited ideas – namely, absolute faith in the beneficence of free markets – continue to guide us. Now more than ever we need new concepts to dissect the social processes through which markets are produced and sustained in practice. This is especially true for markets in labor. It was capitalism’s greatest feat to transform the human capacity to work into a substance that can be bought and sold; while few other transformations have evoked such a backlash from society. To document how work is sustained as a “fictitious commodity” under capitalism constitutes the central task of what we propose to be a critical economic sociology of labor. As with all endeavors, it should commence from first principles.
与其说马克思死而复生，不如说我们这个时代更有可能继续处于“僵尸经济学”（Quiggin 2010）的时代，在这个时代，那些已被经验证伪的观念——即对自由市场益处的绝对信仰——仍然指导着我们。如今，我们比以往任何时候都更需要新的概念来剖析市场在实践中产生和维系的社会过程。这一点在劳动力市场中尤为突出。资本主义最伟大的成就莫过于将人类的劳动能力转化为一种可以买卖的商品；而其他任何变革都很少像它一样引发如此强烈的社会反弹。记录劳动如何在资本主义制度下作为一种“虚构商品”得以维系，构成了我们所提出的批判性劳动经济社会学的核心任务。如同所有事业一样，它应当从基本原理出发。

Labor as Fictitious Commodity
劳动作为一种虚构的商品

It is a foundational anthropological fact that human societies have to find a way to get things done. At some point in the distant future we may all live like the cartoon Jetsons, with robotic butlers to cater to our every need. But even then, actual humans will be needed to design, distribute, update, and repair them. In short, work will always be with us.
人类社会必须找到完成各项事务的方法，这是人类学的一个基本事实。在遥远的未来，我们或许会像动画片《杰森一家》里那样生活，有机器人管家伺候我们的一切需求。但即便如此，仍然需要人类来设计、分发、更新和维修这些机器人。简而言之，工作将永远伴随着我们。

We may give this activity of work a very general definition. It is the process whereby human beings transform the things of the world to create value. These “things” may be material, as when a bitter lemon and granular sugar are stirred into delicious lemonade, or they may be immaterial, as when a composer channels an artistic impulse into a haunting melody. They may even be one’s own body, as when a professional marathoner trains to improve her racing time. Furthermore, the value that is produced through work may derive from the product’s use to the producer, as when that pitcher of lemonade is consumed by the lemonade-maker and his friends for hydration, vitamin C, and pure enjoyment. Or the product of work may be valuable because it can subsequently be exchanged for other things. This would occur if an extra glass from that pitcher of lemonade was sold and the proceeds used to purchase an accompanying pastry.
我们可以将劳动活动赋予一个非常宽泛的定义。它是人类转化世间万物以创造价值的过程。这些“物”可以是物质的，例如将苦柠檬和砂糖搅拌成美味的柠檬水；也可以是非物质的，例如作曲家将艺术灵感融入动人的旋律；甚至可以是自身的身体，例如职业马拉松运动员通过训练来提高比赛成绩。此外，劳动创造的价值可能源于产品对生产者的用途，例如柠檬水制作者和他的朋友们享用这壶柠檬水，补充水分、维生素 C，并享受纯粹的快乐。或者，劳动产品的价值也可能源于其可以与其他物品交换。例如，如果将这壶柠檬水中多余的一杯卖掉，并将所得款项用于购买一份糕点，那么劳动产品就具有了价值。

If the inescapability of work is an anthropological reality, so too is the fact of tremendous variation in how work is accomplished in different places and at different times. Once we widen our gaze from the individual producer to the wider society, a series of questions arise. Who controls the raw materials, or inputs, needed to engage in work, and who can claim ownership of the value, or output, that results? Given that the actual doing of work can be unpleasant, tiresome, and dangerous, what sorts of motivations are needed to get people to engage in production? How are different trades distributed and coordinated across society as a whole? (We certainly would not want a world in which everyone made lemonade for a living.)
如果说工作的不可逃避性是人类学上的现实，那么不同地点、不同时期完成工作的方式存在巨大差异也是不争的事实。一旦我们将目光从个体生产者扩展到更广泛的社会，一系列问题便会随之而来。谁控制着从事工作所需的原材料或投入？谁又能拥有最终产出的价值？鉴于实际工作可能令人不快、疲惫不堪甚至危险，究竟需要什么样的激励才能促使人们参与生产？不同的行业在整个社会中是如何分配和协调的？（我们当然不希望生活在一个人人都靠榨柠檬汁为生的世界里。）

Throughout history, we observe a wide range of answers to such questions. In slave societies such as ancient Greece or the American South up until the nineteenth century, entire classes of people were dehumanized, forced into bondage, and driven to work for a property-owning class. Then there are communal social systems (typically small in scale) in which all members voluntarily take part in work and equally divide among themselves the value that is thereby produced: examples would include an Israeli kibbutz and a non-profit arts festival (Chen 2009). Between these extremes of coercion and cooperation is found capitalism.
纵观历史，我们对这类问题的答案可谓五花八门。在古希腊或 19 世纪之前的美国南方等奴隶制社会中，整个阶层的人都被剥夺了人性，被迫沦为奴隶，为有产阶级卖命。而另一些则是公社式的社会体系（通常规模较小），所有成员自愿参与劳动，并平等地分配由此创造的价值：例如以色列的基布兹和非营利艺术节（Chen 2009）。介于强制与合作这两种极端之间，便是资本主义。

In an ideal-typical capitalist social system, work is organized as marketized employment and legitimated by a series of myths. The first is that modern societies have happened upon the optimal way of organizing production: as a transaction between an employer who supplies the inputs and an employee who creates value from them. Second, the content of the exchange between these two parties can be specified thus: an employee receives financial remuneration in return for her effort. Third, the terms of the exchange (i.e., how much remuneration is provided) are fair. Want to know why your salary is what it is? It’s simply a matter of the market for labor in your trade establishing an equilibrium between demand and supply. As a worker, you decide the lowest, or reservation, wage at which you will accept a job, while employers raise their offers until a clearing price is reached. In sum, by allowing the market to guide the myriad exchanges between workers and employers, capitalism stimulates innovation, properly rewards effort, and creates a prosperous society.
在理想的典型资本主义社会体系中，工作以市场化的雇佣关系形式组织，并由一系列神话赋予其合法性。首先，现代社会偶然发现了组织生产的最佳方式：即雇主提供投入要素，雇员利用这些投入要素创造价值，二者之间进行交易。其次，双方交换的内容可以这样界定：雇员因其付出而获得经济报酬。第三，交换的条件（即报酬的多少）是公平的。想知道你的工资为什么是这个数额吗？这仅仅是你所在行业的劳动力市场在供需之间建立平衡的结果。作为一名劳动者，你可以决定自己能够接受的最低工资，即保留工资，而雇主则会提高工资，直到达到一个平衡价格。总之，通过让市场引导劳资双方之间无数的交换，资本主义刺激了创新，合理地奖励了劳动，并创造了一个繁荣的社会。

Like all gospels, this one has its skeptics. If Marx was the most prominent of the nineteenth century, the title for the twentieth must surely go to the Hungarian social theorist Karl Polanyi (Polanyi-Levitt and Mendell 1987). In his masterpiece, The Great Transformation (2001 [1944]: 29), Polanyi excoriated the “utopian endeavor of economic liberalism to set up a self-regulating market system.” While small-scale markets had existed throughout human history, it was only with the advent of modern capitalism that three key substances – land, money, and labor – were brought into the market system. The problem, however, is that natural resources, symbolic currencies, and human creativities are not created to serve as commodities. When you systematically price, buy, and sell them, you distort and ultimately destroy them. For this reason, Polanyi referred to land, money, and labor as fictitious commodities whose uprooting from traditional social structures will generate backlash and counter-movements.
如同所有福音书一样，这套理论也难逃质疑。如果说马克思是十九世纪最杰出的思想家，那么二十世纪的领军人物无疑是匈牙利社会理论家卡尔·波兰尼（Polanyi-Levitt and Mendell 1987）。在他的代表作《大转型》（2001 [1944]: 29）中，波兰尼猛烈抨击了“经济自由主义建立自我调节市场体系的乌托邦式尝试”。尽管小规模市场在人类历史上一直存在，但直到现代资本主义出现，土地、货币和劳动力这三种关键要素才被纳入市场体系。然而，问题在于，自然资源、象征性货币和人类创造力并非天生就该作为商品存在。当你系统地对它们进行定价、买卖时，你就会扭曲它们，最终摧毁它们。因此，波兰尼将土地、金钱和劳动力称为虚构的商品，认为将它们从传统的社会结构中拔除将会引发反弹和反动运动。

Extending Polanyi’s original insights, economic sociologists have documented how, precisely, markets are constituted as social structures (Bandelj 2009b; Beckert 2002; Fligstein 1996; Nee 1998; Sutton et al. 1994). The general story is that proponents of economic liberalism seek to extend the logic of the market into new arenas; opponents of this logic push back; and from this dialectic there emerge unique hybrids of economic and non-economic practices. In other words, unlike the textbook model of markets as spaces wherein supply meets demand via self-interested transacting, real-world markets remain embedded in a variety of domains collectively dubbed “the social” (Storper and Salais 1997).
经济社会学家在波兰尼的原创洞见基础上，详细记录了市场如何构成社会结构（Bandelj 2009b; Beckert 2002; Fligstein 1996; Nee 1998; Sutton et al. 1994）。总体而言，经济自由主义的支持者试图将市场逻辑扩展到新的领域；反对者则予以反击；而正是在这种辩证关系中，经济实践与非经济实践的独特混合体应运而生。换言之，与教科书中将市场视为供需通过自利交易进行匹配的空间的模式不同，现实世界的市场始终嵌入于各种统称为“社会”的领域之中（Storper and Salais 1997）。

Three of these domains in particular deserve attention: the cultural, the collective, and the cognitive. By the cultural, economic sociologists refer to the innate tendency for human groups to imbue economic phenomena (such as acts of production and exchange) with meaning via rich symbolic systems. Moral obligations, collective narratives, and aesthetic principles are just some of the cultural process that can be said to ground markets in the social. By collective embeddedness we refer to the social structures in which market participants are located and which shape their behavior in ways that cannot be described by methodological individualism. Examples here range from political fields to interpersonal networks to small-group situations. Finally, the notion of cognitive embeddedness captures how and why behavior departs from the predictions of doctrinaire rational choice theories. Biographical experience, decision-making heuristics, and even one’s religious faith have all been shown to influence the choices made in markets.
其中三个领域尤其值得关注：文化领域、集体领域和认知领域。经济社会学家所说的文化领域，指的是人类群体通过丰富的符号系统赋予经济现象（例如生产和交换行为）意义的内在倾向。道德义务、集体叙事和审美原则只是将市场与社会联系起来的文化过程的一部分。我们所说的集体嵌入性，指的是市场参与者所处的社会结构，这些结构以方法论上的个人主义无法描述的方式塑造着他们的行为。这方面的例子包括政治领域、人际网络以及小群体情境。最后，认知嵌入性的概念揭示了行为如何以及为何偏离教条式的理性选择理论的预测。个人经历、决策启发式方法，甚至宗教信仰，都被证明会影响人们在市场中的选择。

Equipped with this theoretical toolkit, economic sociologists have documented the social structuring of a wide variety of markets: dollars and pesos, kidneys and cotton, rain forests and fire insurance, sperm and eggs, and on and on. Relatively neglected within this research program, however, has been the question of how markets in human labor are sustained. This is curious insofar as labor was one of the original members of Polanyi’s triad of fictitious commodities. Why did labor disappear from the research agenda? It’s hard to say exactly, but the effect has been to cede the subject to labor process scholars, industrial psychologists, human resources specialists, behavioral economists, inequality specialists, and organization ethnographers. What is lacking, and what this volume provides, is a single source that ties together key theoretical insights of economic sociology with the impressive empirical studies produced by these latter literatures.
凭借这套理论工具，经济社会学家记录了各种市场的社会结构：美元和比索、肾脏和棉花、雨林和火灾保险、精子和卵子等等。然而，在这一研究领域，关于人类劳动市场如何维系的问题却相对被忽视了。这令人费解，因为劳动是波兰尼最初提出的“虚构商品三元组”之一。为什么劳动从研究议程中消失了？很难确切地说，但其结果是，这一主题被劳动过程学者、工业心理学家、人力资源专家、行为经济学家、不平等问题专家和组织民族志学者所占据。本书所缺乏的，正是将经济社会学的关键理论见解与后者文献中令人瞩目的实证研究联系起来的单一来源。

Overview of the Argument
论证概述

The structure of the argument differs from that of a standard sociology textbook. The latter is typically organized according to the themes that dominate public discourse on employment and its problems. And so we find chapters on unemployment, job satisfaction, management styles, the service sector, strike activity, and so on. While all of these topics will be addressed in the pages to follow, the chapters themselves derive from our central thesis: that markets for labor, the penultimate fictitious commodity, are social structures. Indeed, an implicit goal of this book is to encourage readers to think critically about the accepted, “common-sense” categories through which work is commonly discussed and evaluated.
本书的论证结构与标准社会学教科书有所不同。后者通常根据主导公共就业及其问题讨论的主题进行组织，因此我们会看到关于失业、工作满意度、管理风格、服务业、罢工活动等等的章节。虽然所有这些主题都将在接下来的篇幅中得到探讨，但各章节本身都源于我们的核心论点：劳动力市场——这个几乎等同于虚构商品的市场——是一种社会结构。事实上，本书的一个隐含目标是鼓励读者批判性地思考那些被普遍接受的、被视为“常识”的、用于讨论和评价工作的范畴。

The initial two chapters provide a primer on the historical and cultural foundations of commodified labor. Chapter 2, “The Great Transformation of Work,” discusses how formal employment came to supplant other ways of creating value as the normative form of work during the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries. Rejecting the technological determinism that underlies standard accounts of an “industrial revolution,” it emphasizes how advances in technology, machinery, and industry were part and parcel of larger shifts in governance, organization, and culture. It also summarizes the work of several classical theorists notable for how they situated everyday practices of work in relation to these macro-shifts. These include Adam Smith, a philosopher of the Scottish Enlightenment who elaborated an intellectual and moral justification for market society; Marx, who believed that the exploitation underlying marketized labor would eventually drive workers to overthrow capitalism; early sociologists such as Max Weber, Georg Simmel, and Émile Durkheim, all of whom believed that capitalism could overcome its various pathologies; and Polanyi, who argued that free markets in labor are a fiction requiring constant management lest they spontaneously combust.
前两章概述了商品化劳动的历史和文化基础。第二章“劳动的伟大转型”探讨了18世纪和19世纪正规就业如何取代其他创造价值的方式，成为劳动的规范形式。本章驳斥了“工业革命”标准论述中常见的技术决定论，强调技术、机械和工业的进步是治理、组织和文化等更大变革不可分割的一部分。本章还总结了几位古典理论家的著作，他们尤其关注如何将日常劳动实践与这些宏观变革联系起来。这些理论家包括苏格兰启蒙运动时期的哲学家亚当·斯密，他为市场社会提供了理论和道德上的辩护；马克思，他认为市场化劳动背后存在的剥削最终会驱使工人推翻资本主义；以及早期社会学家，如马克斯·韦伯、格奥尔格·齐美尔和埃米尔·涂尔干，他们都认为资本主义能够克服其各种弊病。波兰尼则认为，劳动力自由市场是一种虚构，需要不断管理，否则就会自燃。

Chapter 3, “Classifying Labor,” argues that capitalism depends upon the stabilization of market employment as a valid cultural category. The stay-at-home parent, the drug dealer, and the volunteer all transform the world in a way that can be said to add value to it. They all work. But capitalism recognizes as legitimate only employment in the labor market for a wage. That this narrow vision of work is not natural in the order of things can be surmised from the unending disputes within capitalist societies over who precisely counts as a worker. Much is at stake in these boundary disputes. If your labor is classified as employment, you achieve certain rights (such as the ability to unionize) but also certain responsibilities (such as the obligation to pay taxes on your earnings). Ultimately, it is the state that possesses the authority to validate such classifications, raising the issue of how cultural meanings, economic interests, and governance regimes intersect.
第三章“劳动分类”论证了资本主义依赖于市场就业作为一种有效的文化范畴的稳定化。全职父母、毒贩和志愿者都以某种方式改变了世界，可以说他们都为世界增添了价值。他们都在工作。但资本主义只承认在劳动力市场中获得工资的就业是合法的。这种狭隘的劳动观并非自然而然，这一点可以从资本主义社会内部关于谁才算作“劳动者”的无休止争论中看出。这些边界之争事关重大。如果你的劳动被归类为就业，你就能获得某些权利（例如加入工会的权利），同时也承担某些责任（例如缴纳收入税款的义务）。最终，国家拥有确认此类分类的权威，这就引出了一个问题：文化意义、经济利益和治理机制是如何相互交织的。

The next two chapters elucidate the social mechanisms necessary to accomplish wage labor in practice. Chapter 4, “Commensurating Labor,” asks of employment a question relevant to all types of exchange: how is an equivalency established between substance A and substance B? Under capitalism, an employer and an employee (as well as the institutions that regulate the employment relationship) must come to an (often tacit) agreement as to what exactly is being exchanged when the former hires the latter. A simple equation such as wage = labor belies the tremendous variation that research has shown to exist across and within societies as to how the employment exchange is conceptualized. In addition, once labor becomes a commodity, it must be priced. Determining the worth of this particular commodity, however, is not a simple matter of letting the magic of the market do its work. In practice, cultural standards come into play, as illustrated by such issues as minimum versus living wages, and free-trade versus fair-trade principles.
接下来的两章阐述了在实践中实现雇佣劳动所必需的社会机制。第四章“劳动的等价性”提出了一个与所有类型的交换都相关的就业问题：物质 A 和物质 B 之间如何建立等价关系？在资本主义制度下，雇主和雇员（以及规范雇佣关系的机构）必须就雇主雇用雇员时究竟交换了什么达成（通常是默契的）协议。诸如工资=劳动这样简单的等式掩盖了研究表明在不同社会之间以及同一社会内部，人们对就业交换的理解存在的巨大差异。此外，一旦劳动成为一种商品，就必须对其进行定价。然而，确定这种特定商品的价值并非仅仅是让市场发挥作用那么简单。在实践中，文化标准会发挥作用，例如最低工资与生活工资、自由贸易与公平贸易原则等问题。

Chapter 5, “Making Labor Markets,” broadens our scope beyond the employer–employee dyad. It interprets through an economic sociological lens a vibrant stream of research documenting the mechanisms through which firms find workers, and vice versa. There can be little doubt that real-world labor markets scarcely resemble those supposed by economic models. Every time an elected official caves into xenophobic sentiment by restricting the inflow of skilled immigrants, a manager turns down a qualified job applicant because of the color of her skin, or a job-seeker secures employment through the referral of a distant uncle, we witness the social structuring of the labor market. In addition, labor markets are excellent sites to examine contestations over commodified labor. States, unions, social movements, and others all debate the relative merits of market-based versus traditional mechanisms of employer–employee matching.
第五章“劳动力市场的构建”将我们的视野拓展到雇主与雇员关系之外。它运用经济社会学的视角，解读了大量研究成果，这些研究记录了企业寻找员工以及员工寻找雇主的机制。毋庸置疑，现实世界的劳动力市场与经济模型所设想的截然不同。每当一位民选官员屈服于排外情绪，限制技术移民的流入；每当一位经理因为肤色而拒绝一位合格的求职者；每当一位求职者通过远房叔叔的推荐找到工作时，我们都目睹了劳动力市场的社会结构化过程。此外，劳动力市场也是研究劳动力商品化争议的绝佳场所。各州、工会、社会运动以及其他各方都在就基于市场的雇佣匹配机制与传统雇佣匹配机制的优劣展开辩论。

The remainder of the book puts front and center the issue of contested commodification. Chapter 6, “Controlling Labor,” critically interrogates the assumption that wage labor is a consensual exchange between coequals. For various reasons, employees may resist attempts to extract from them a certain amount or type of work at a certain price. For the employer, this introduces the problem of management: that is, of monitoring and controlling the exchange. Many assume this is a simple matter of providing the proper mix of incentives and disincentives. In reality, however, employers in capitalist economies regularly use non-market mechanisms to extract work from employees. This chapter summarizes three such strategies: a reliance upon gift exchange rather than monetary payment to induce effort; the recruitment of workers whose early socialization predisposes them to perform a certain sort of labor; and the structuring of work processes as engrossing games the mastery of which legitimizes labor’s commodification.
本书余下部分将争议性的商品化问题置于核心位置。第六章“控制劳动力”对“工资劳动是平等双方之间自愿交换”这一假设提出了批判性质疑。出于各种原因，雇员可能会抵制以特定价格从他们身上榨取一定数量或类型劳动的企图。对雇主而言，这就引出了管理问题：即如何监控和控制这种交换。许多人认为，这仅仅是提供适当激励和惩罚机制组合的问题。然而，在现实中，资本主义经济体中的雇主经常使用非市场机制来榨取雇员的劳动。本章总结了三种这样的策略：依靠礼物交换而非金钱支付来激励劳动；招募那些早期社会化经历使其倾向于从事某种特定类型劳动的工人；以及将工作流程构建成引人入胜的游戏，而掌握这些游戏则使劳动商品化合法化。

Chapter 7, “Labor and Group-Making,” challenges the assumption that a capitalist labor market is an aggregate of myriad exchanges between individual buyers and sellers of labor. Quite often, both parties to the exchange act collectively rather than individually. The resulting collectivities can exhibit varying degrees of formalization. On the employer side they can range from implicit (and often illicit) understandings to established business associations, and on the employee side they can extend from informal work groups to trade union federations. In this chapter we examine the various bases of power that can facilitate or inhibit collective action in the employment market context.
第七章“劳动与群体构建”挑战了资本主义劳动力市场是由无数个体买卖双方之间的交易构成的这一假设。很多时候，交易双方并非单独行动，而是集体行动。由此产生的集体组织形式多样，正式程度也各不相同。在雇主方面，集体组织的形式从隐性（且往往非法）的默契到正式的商业协会不等；在雇员方面，集体组织的形式则从非正式的工作小组到工会联合会不等。本章将探讨在就业市场环境中，哪些权力基础能够促进或阻碍集体行动。

A concluding chapter, “What Good is Embeddeness?,” ponders the future of labor under capitalism. The notion of embeddedness, we argue, proved quite useful for analyzing how labor was socially structured under systems of regulated capitalism. However, the terrain has shifted. Financialization, globalization, and digitization have drastically increased the relative bargaining power of employers vis-à-vis labor. At the same time, governments have opted to withdraw social supports for citizens and protections for workers. In addition to documenting this disembedding of labor, a critical economic sociology should seek to theorize the conditions under which labor can be reembedded in the social.
最后一章“嵌入性有何益处？”探讨了资本主义下劳动的未来。我们认为，嵌入性概念在分析规制资本主义体系下劳动的社会结构方面非常有用。然而，形势已经发生了变化。金融化、全球化和数字化极大地增强了雇主相对于劳动者的议价能力。与此同时，各国政府选择削减对公民的社会支持和对劳动者的保护。除了记录这种劳动的脱离嵌入性之外，批判性的经济社会学还应致力于构建劳动重新嵌入社会的理论框架。

The Great Transformation of Work
工作的重大变革

Work – the creation of value through the application of effort – has always been with us. But employment has not. Throughout most of recorded history, humans have produced the things they need and want outside of the context of wage work in a labor market. A capitalist world of work in fact has relatively recent origins. These are to be found in a series of developments that took place in Europe during the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries, and which soon spread to encompass the entire globe. The common label for this process, which is that of an “industrial revolution,” too narrowly focuses upon its technical and economic dimensions. Following Karl Polanyi (2001 [1944]), we understand the current system of global capitalism as the product of a “great transformation” that revolutionized not only industry, but also political, organizational, and cultural life (Smelser 1959).
劳动——即通过付出努力创造价值——一直伴随着我们。但就业并非如此。在有记载的历史的大部分时间里，人类生产所需所想之物并非源于劳动力市场的雇佣劳动。事实上，资本主义的劳动世界起源相对较晚。其根源可以追溯到 18、19 世纪欧洲发生的一系列发展，这些发展很快便遍及全球。人们通常将这一过程称为“工业革命”，但这种说法过于狭隘地关注其技术和经济层面。借鉴卡尔·波兰尼（Karl Polanyi，2001 [1944]）的观点，我们认为当前的全球资本主义体系是“大转型”的产物，这场转型不仅彻底改变了工业，也彻底改变了政治、组织和文化生活（Smelser，1959）。

The purpose of this chapter is not to provide a comprehensive history of this transformation. 1^{1} Rather, it is to lay the foundation for an economic-sociological critique of the assumption, dominant in the world today, that work is synonymous with the market exchange of labor. To do so, we reconstruct a debate among four theoretical traditions (the Smithian, Marxist, sociological, and Polanyian) concerning three key aspects of the great transformation (its origins, consequences, and future).
