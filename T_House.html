The proportion of U.S. workers employed in service industries has grown steadily throughout this century (from 30% in 1900, to 50% in 1950, to 75% today). Sociologists and scholars of the workplace, however, did not begin to theorize how this transition affected our traditional, manufacturing-based understandings of labor relations until the past few decades. Specifically, they have argued that the immediate presence of clients at the point of production complicates managerial strategies for organizing the labor process and monitoring/disciplining workers. These dilemmas have been described as being of two main types: those of worker autonomy and interests.

Regarding autonomy, researchers have pointed out the possibilities of increased worker surveillance and control offered by the presence of clients. Considering that legal gambling has throughout the majority of U.S. history been confined to a single, sparsely populated state, a surprisingly large amount of primary and secondary source materials exist on the work experiences of Nevada’s casino dealers throughout the 20th Century. My historical analysis in this article is thus based on a series of oral histories of early Nevada casino workers collected by historians at the University of Nevada, several ethnographies of casino dealing, management publications, employee handbooks, and my own interviews with veteran casino workers.
考虑到美国历史上合法赌博大多局限于一个人口稀少的州，关于20世纪内华达州赌场荷官的工作经历，却有数量惊人的第一手和第二手资料。因此，本文的历史分析基于内华达大学历史学家收集的一系列早期内华达州赌场员工的口述历史、几部关于赌场荷官的民族志、管理出版物、员工手册以及我本人对资深赌场员工的访谈。

The ethnographic portion of the article, in turn, is based on a 4-month stint as a casino dealer. In late spring, I moved to Nevada and, as must all aspiring croupiers today, enrolled in a state-licensed vocational school offering standardized courses in dealing. Completion of such a program constitutes a prerequisite for applying to dealing jobs. 6^{6} In June (a peak in the hiring season), I applied to the Jackpot Corporation as a blackjack dealer. I specifically chose Jackpot as a potential research site because of its current structural position in the U.S. casino industry. The prevailing image of the contemporary casino is still that of the glamorous, Disney-esque, megacasino on the Las Vegas Strip catering to a high-rolling, often international clientele. Both Leidner (1999) and Hochschild (1983) have argued that as competition within service industries intensifies, customer service becomes an increasingly important mechanism of product differentiation. To attract and retain clients, management organizes the labor process to procure emotional labor from workers. This formulation, on the surface, fits the case of Nevada’s casino firms. As the industry has moved from a monopoly to a competitive structure, the labor process for dealers has become increasingly geared toward service provision and now involves considerable worker autonomy. Yet the current configuration of worker and managerial practices on the casino floor is not explained through reference to new service demands alone. One must also consider both changes in the management structure of the firm—especially the decoupling of corporate and shop-floor management—and the full range of corporate responses to competition—both the technical “re-skilling” of workers and attempts to cut labor costs.
莱德纳（Leidner，1999）和霍赫希尔德（Hochschild，1983）都认为，随着服务行业竞争加剧，客户服务已成为产品差异化日益重要的机制。为了吸引和留住客户，管理层会组织劳动过程，以获取员工的情感劳动。表面上看，这种观点似乎符合内华达州赌场公司的案例。随着该行业从垄断结构转向竞争结构，荷官的劳动过程越来越倾向于提供服务，并赋予员工相当大的自主权。然而，仅凭新的服务需求并不能解释赌场当前员工和管理实践的现状。我们还必须考虑公司管理结构的变化——尤其是公司总部与一线管理的脱钩——以及公司应对竞争的各种措施——包括对员工进行技术“再培训”和削减劳动力成本的尝试。

The history of legal gambling in Nevada has been one of a progression in the casinos’ primary profit strategy from security to speed to service. During the 1970s and 1980s, rapid increases in both supply of and demand for gambling—coupled with the state’s national monopoly on the product—led to a second, boom era in the Nevada casino industry’s history. The past decade has witnessed the onset of a third, competitive period in the history of the Nevada casino industry (Bear, Stearns and Co., 2001; Eadington, 1999; Marfels, 1995, 1999). On one hand, the industry’s monopolistic structure was abruptly altered in the late 1980s when South Dakota and Iowa legalized casino gambling and Congress passed the Indian Gaming Regulatory Act (Eadington, 1990). All allowed casinos in non-Nevada locales and were shortly followed by similar measures elsewhere. As expected, with this transformation in industry structure from monopoly to competitive conditions have come new attempts by firms to differentiate their “product” from other casinos’ by marketing to consumers not just the opportunity to gamble but a unique “gaming experience” (Vogel, 1994). The house also regulates seven aspects of the interactive component of dealing—how croupiers converse with customers and each other. First, to maximize speed, dealers must teach novice players the procedures of play so they do not unnecessarily hold up the game. This education, though, must involve only rules of play, not game strategy. Computer simulations have determined the “proper” blackjack strategy that maximizes the player’s win percentage. 13^{13} It allows the player to win nearly half (49%) of the time. The average player, though, plays around the 35% to 45% level (Vogel, 1994). Dealers of course know the basics of the proper strategy but are prohibited from advising players on how to play their hands to maximize security (i.e., the house advantage on individual hands and thus the overall house edge). Third, dealers must constantly monitor their tables for possible cheating and report any unusual player behavior to the floor supervisor. THE DAILY GRIND: AUTONOMY FOR LOW-ROLLERS
日常琐事：低收入者的自主生活

Low-rollers (those who play $3 to $20 hands) constituted around 90% of Jackpot’s clientele. During many shifts, in fact, a dealer would only encounter low-rollers. And although corporate management directs dealers to provide service to and ensure security for all players, pit bosses care only about maximizing speed for low-rollers. They thus here withdrew control, allowing the logic of the tipping system to regulate the labor process. For just as pit bosses seek to maximize the overall volume of low-rollers’ small-stake wagers, dealers understand that more hands dealt means more tipping opportunities and therefore spontaneously maximize their speed. Players would often complain about the pace at which we dealt or even ask us to slow down. Invariably a dealer would respond, “Sorry, but we have to deal this way” or “Can’t help it. The hand is quicker than the eye.” Consent was thus secured as well over the intense routinization of dealing work. At no point during my fieldwork did I hear workers complain about the severe standardization of their every move, for on the shop floor itself, dealing according to procedure was understood as—and as far as I could tell was—the fastest way to deal and thus to make tips.
小额玩家（每手牌下注 3 到 20 美元的玩家）约占 Jackpot 赌场客户群的 90%。事实上，很多时候，荷官遇到的都是小额玩家。尽管公司管理层指示荷官要为所有玩家提供服务并确保安全，但赌场主管只关心如何最大限度地提高小额玩家的出牌速度。因此，他们放弃了对小额玩家的控制权，任由小费制度的逻辑来调节工作流程。正如赌场主管力求最大化小额玩家小额投注的总量一样，荷官也明白，发牌越多，获得小费的机会就越多，因此他们会自发地加快发牌速度。玩家经常抱怨我们发牌的速度，甚至要求我们放慢速度。荷官总是回答：“抱歉，我们必须这样发牌”或“没办法，手速比眼速快”。就这样，发牌工作高度程式化，玩家们也逐渐接受了这种模式。在我的实地考察过程中，我从未听到工人们抱怨他们的每一个动作都被严格标准化，因为在车间里，按照程序行事被认为是——而且据我所知也确实是——最快的处理方式，因此也是获得小费最快的方式。

Yet the flip side of the maximum-speed performance generated by the tipping system is sacrificed security and service provision. The toke bet system, that is, creates incentives both to assist players who are tipping and to treat nontippers rudely to force them off. Having made players aware of the toke system, dealers must make clear that tips are not mere gratuities or gifts but rather a fee for a service rendered. They will, for instance, whisper advice to players to emphasize that such hints are not only not a part of their job duties but against the rules as well. And when an initial tip is offered, a dealer’s demeanor changes dramatically from indifference to active engagement with the action on the table.
在向玩家解释了小费制度后，荷官必须明确指出，小费并非馈赠或礼物，而是对所提供服务的报酬。例如，他们会悄悄地向玩家暗示，强调此类暗示不仅不属于他们的工作职责，而且也违反了规则。当玩家首次提出小费时，荷官的态度会从漠不关心转变为积极参与到牌桌上的游戏中。

COMMUNICATING INFORMATION TO OTHER DEALERS

Information on player tipping behavior is not only collected but shared among workers as well. Workers’ tactics for dealing with and to players they have labeled tippers fall into two broad categories: helping them win (“improving their luck”) and providing extra emotional labor (“treating them right”). When executed successfully, they both enable (in terms of the first tactic) and encourage (in terms of the second) players to continue tipping. Blackjack dealers can help players win in two ways. First, they can offer advice on basic blackjack strategy. Splits 18^{18} are key opportunities to offer assistance.

More generally, dealers can advise players whether to hit their hand.

To offer assistance, the dealer must deploy a minitactic to see the player’s cards. The dealer can crane her or his neck to catch a glimpse of the player’s hand; she or he can, with varying degrees of subtlety, ask the player for this information; or the player himself or herself can verbally or visually convey it:
为了提供帮助，荷官必须运用一些小技巧来查看玩家的牌。荷官可以伸长脖子瞥一眼玩家的手牌；也可以用不同程度的委婉方式询问玩家；或者玩家自己也可以通过口头或眼神的方式表达出来：

It is late in the night and I am dealing to Troy, an outgoing salesman from Los Angeles. He is a tipper and puts me up frequently, referring to “us winning.” But I soon realize that he is not the smartest of players and so help him out as best I can. He begins turning his cards face up on the table when he receives them—so I will be able to see his hand—and giving me ambiguous hit signals. I respond by doing everything I can to assist him, practically playing the hands for him. “We’re good there,” I say. “That 13’s gonna need some help.” Eventually the pit boss notices, strolls over and tells Troy to stop exposing his cards, casting me a dirty look as well. While this reprimand would have scared me early in my fieldwork, I now merely tone down my assistance. The exchange of tips—dollar chips for helpful hints—continues, albeit more covertly.
夜已深，我正在给特洛伊发牌，他是个来自洛杉矶的外向推销员。他出手阔绰，经常给我小费，还说“我们赢了”。但我很快意识到他牌技并不精湛，于是尽我所能地帮助他。他拿到牌后就开始把牌面朝上放在桌上——这样我就能看到他的底牌——还给我一些模棱两可的要牌信号。我则竭尽所能地帮助他，几乎是替他打牌。“我们这局没问题，”我说，“那张13点牌需要点帮助。” 后来，赌场主管注意到了，走过来让特洛伊别再亮牌，还狠狠地瞪了我一眼。如果是在我实习初期，这种训斥肯定会吓到我，但现在我只是稍微收敛了些。小费的交换——用筹码换取有用的提示——仍在继续，只是更加隐蔽了。

The second way that dealers can help tippers is to pay attention to the cards that have been played from the deck and time their shuffle to increase the player’s odds of winning. Whereas official procedure dictates that the deck be shuffled only when exactly two thirds of the way through, if after the first hand the deck is disadvantageous to players (i.e., a disproportionately high number of 10s have been played), a dealer can shuffle early. Conversely, she or he can shuffle later than procedure dictates when the deck is favorable to players. If, however, despite all attempts to the contrary, a dealer is “hot” and unable to lose, she or he can still go to great lengths to emphasize that she or he is, in fact, on the player’s side and as much a victim of bad luck as they:
荷官帮助玩家获得小费的第二种方法是关注牌组中已打出的牌，并把握洗牌时机，以提高玩家的胜率。官方规定牌组必须在三分之二的牌局结束后洗牌，但如果第一局结束后牌组对玩家不利（例如，10点牌的数量过多），荷官可以提前洗牌。反之，如果牌组对玩家有利，荷官也可以在规则规定的时间之后洗牌。然而，如果荷官手气正旺，无论如何努力都无法输牌，他/她仍然可以竭尽全力强调自己其实是站在玩家这边的，和玩家一样也是运气不佳的受害者：

After work tonight I take $50 of my tip money and go to play blackjack at a neighboring casino with a coworker. We immediately make our status as tippers known, and the dealer, a young Asian man, responds by cracking jokes and laughing with us. He keeps turning up blackjacks and 20s however, so our tip bets go into the chip tray [to the House]. I see that he is continually breaking the deck early, but to no avail. With each winning hand, he looks increasingly distraught and says apologetically, “I’m trying! I’m trying!”
今晚下班后，我拿着50美元的小费和一位同事去隔壁赌场玩二十一点。我们立刻表明了自己是给小费的，荷官是一位年轻的亚裔男子，他开玩笑地和我们一起笑。然而，他总是翻出黑杰克和20点，所以我们的小费都进了筹码盘（进了赌场）。我看到他一直在提前发牌，但还是没能赢。每赢一局，他看起来就越来越沮丧，连忙道歉说：“我尽力了！我尽力了！”

EMOTIONAL LABOR FOR TIPPERS
小费者的情感劳动

The second type of tactic is the provision of extra emotional labor for the tipper, the most basic unit of which is the smile:
第二种策略是为给小费的人提供额外的情感劳动，其中最基本的单位就是微笑：

I am talking before work with Teresa, a rather shy dealer from Mexico. “To make tips,” she tells me, “you have to please the customer.” “Well, how do you do that?” I ask. “You smile at them.”
上班前，我和特蕾莎聊天，她是一位来自墨西哥、性格比较腼腆的销售员。“要想拿到小费，”她告诉我，“就得让顾客满意。”“那该怎么做呢？”我问。“对他们微笑就行了。”

In addition, dealers “read” players and offer appropriate emotional displays depending on their age, gender, general disposition, and so on. In general, female, young, and grind players were offered informal, relaxed, and “fun” forms of service. Dealers also personalize their interactions with tippers more than with nontippers. They ask and address them by their first names, inquire about where they are from and what they do, and so on. They refer to the tipper’s play as a joint enterprise: “We almost got that one,” “Where’s that 5 when we need it?” And they appear to be more emotionally invested in game outcomes than they actually are by smiling or laughing when players win and frowning when they lose.
荷官与给小费的玩家互动时，会比对待不给小费的玩家更加个性化。他们会直呼玩家的名字，询问他们的籍贯、职业等等。他们会把给小费玩家的牌局视为共同的事业：“我们差点就赢了”，“我们需要那张5点牌的时候，它在哪儿呢？”而且，当玩家赢牌时，荷官会面带微笑或大笑；而当玩家输牌时，荷官则会皱眉，这似乎表明他们对牌局结果的投入程度远超实际情况。

Older players and men playing alone, in contrast, are more serious about their game play and expect reserved, deferential, and status-enhancing emotional labor. Maria, a Hispanic blackjack dealer in her 20s, explains one of her tactics for these players:
相比之下，年纪较大的玩家和独自玩牌的男性玩家则更加认真对待游戏，他们期望得到含蓄、恭敬且能提升自身地位的情感付出。玛丽亚是一位二十多岁的西班牙裔二十一点荷官，她解释了自己针对这类玩家的一种策略：

“You know how these old guys are. They like to be taken care of. So you pay attention to what he’s drinking and when the cocktail waitress comes around, if I see he needs another drink, I’ll go ahead and order his drink for him.” This impresses him, she thinks, and encourages more tips.
“你知道这些老家伙都喜欢被人照顾。所以你要留意他喝什么，等调酒师过来，如果我看到他还需要酒，我就帮他点一杯。”她觉得这样能让他印象深刻，也能让他多给些小费。

More generally, the dealer calls these players by their last names. During the game, she or he “takes care of them” by watching and learning their basic game strategy to then assist them with it. And the dealer makes their play seem central to table outcomes. For instance, if there is only one such tipper at a table full of players, the dealer, on busting, can say to him or her, “Good play, sir. That’ll get everyone paid,” regardless of whether his or her play had been the deciding factor in breaking the dealer. At a table packed full of players, all of whom are following the game closely, the difference in a dealer’s treatment of a tipper vis-à-vis others is both conspicuous to all and a sure source of additional toke bets.
更普遍的情况是，荷官会称呼这些玩家的姓氏。在游戏过程中，荷官会观察并学习他们的基本游戏策略，从而“照顾”他们，并协助他们。荷官还会让他们的打法显得对牌桌结果至关重要。例如，如果一张满桌的玩家中只有一位这样的“小费玩家”，那么在爆牌时，荷官可以对他说：“打得好，先生。这下大家都能赢钱了。”无论这位玩家的打法是否是导致荷官爆牌的决定性因素。在一张挤满了玩家的牌桌上，所有玩家都密切关注着游戏，荷官对待小费玩家与其他玩家的不同态度不仅显而易见，而且肯定会带来额外的下注。

FORCING OFF NONTIPPERS
强制不倾倒垃圾者

When dealing to stiffs, however, dealers conspire to force them off the table as quickly as possible to make room for players who tip. Dealers are landlords, I was told, and nontippers tenants who do not pay the rent:
然而，当遇到吝啬的玩家时，荷官们会串通起来，尽快把他们赶下牌桌，好给那些愿意给小费的玩家腾出位置。有人告诉我，荷官就像房东，而不给小费的玩家就像不交房租的房客。

You know how some players be sitting there for three or four hours at a time but never put down a bet for you? You go to break, come back and they’re still there. No offense. Sit down and play a few hands, but don’t be taking up that seat all night long if you ain’t gonna pay some rent. (Hugo, a 23-year-old Hispanic man)
你知道有些玩家一坐就是三四个小时，却从来不给你下注吗？你去休息，回来发现他们还在那儿。别介意。坐下来玩几手牌就行，但如果你不打算付房租，就别整晚都占着那个位置。（雨果，23岁的西班牙裔男子）

Dealers attempt to force out these players by making them lose. They can, for example, simply follow procedure by withholding assistance they would normally grant even the most modest tipper. They merely say nothing when the player splits 10s or does not split 8s. And they respond to requests for advice by saying, “That’s a tough call” or “I don’t know . . . are you feeling lucky?”
荷官会想方设法让这些玩家输钱，以此逼他们出局。例如，他们可以按照惯例，拒绝提供通常即使是给小费最少的玩家也会提供的帮助。当玩家分牌10点或不分牌8点时，他们只会保持沉默。而当玩家寻求建议时，他们的回应往往是：“这很难说”或者“我不知道……你今天运气好吗？”

Dealers can also bend the house rules slightly by altering their shuffle (i.e., dealing sooner or later than procedure dictates) to change a bad run of the cards (here, one that is going well for the player). Joanne, my first-night “trainer,” introduced me to this technique of “shuffling your shuffle”:
荷官还可以通过稍微改变洗牌顺序（例如，比规定时间更早或更晚发牌）来稍微变通一下赌场规则，从而扭转不利的牌局（在这里，指的是对玩家有利的牌局）。我第一晚的“培训师”乔安妮向我介绍了这种“改变洗牌顺序”的技巧：

It is late in the evening and the players at my table are winning big, but not tipping. Joanne tells me to change my shuffle, “Take a third from the top or shuffle an extra time. It doesn’t matter. Just do something different.” I’m not quite sure why she is saying this, but I assume it is because I am doing something wrong. On the next break, though, she explains, “They were winning but they weren’t tipping. You got to change the flow of the cards, try to break them up a bit.”
夜已深，我桌上的玩家赢了不少钱，却都不给小费。乔安娜让我换个洗牌方式，“从牌堆顶拿三分之一的牌，或者多洗一次。都行，反正就是换个洗牌方式。” 我不太明白她为什么这么说，但我猜是因为我哪里做错了。不过，在下一次休息时，她解释说：“他们赢了钱却不给小费。你得改变一下发牌的节奏，试着把牌打乱一点。”

And once dealers do find a good run of cards (here, one that is going poorly for the player), they simply concentrate on dealing extremely rapidly, knowing that after all, the odds are in their (and the house’s) favor. In the words of Kim, a veteran dealer, “Just find a sweet spot in the deck and wipe ’em out.”
一旦荷官拿到一手好牌（在这种情况下，对玩家非常不利），他们就会集中精力快速发牌，因为他们知道，毕竟胜算在他们（和庄家）这边。正如资深荷官金所说：“只要找到牌堆里的最佳位置，然后把牌全部清空就行了。”

CONCLUSION

I entered the casino equipped with the conceptual tools of the new service work literature, especially Hochschild’s (1983) “emotional labor” and Leidner’s (1999) “worker-client-management interest alliance.” And although they were immensely helpful for describing the dynamics of the dealing labor process, I found them inadequate for situating the labor process within both the structure of the firm itself and historical changes in its organizational environment. I thus returned to the manufacturing literature. Specifically, I drew on Burawoy’s (1998) notion of “hegemonic regime” to describe the historical genesis of Jackpot’s method of labor control (a decoupling of management structures and technical re-skilling of dealing labor), how workers experience it (as a “game of making tips”), and its larger functions for the firm (customized service provision and lower labor costs). By so doing, my study achieves a broad generalizability by allowing the reconstruction of prevailing theories of the labor process in the contemporary service economy. In addition, however, I will conclude by addressing how the findings of this study may be directly applied to a more general category of service workers: tipped employees.
我带着新服务业文献中的概念工具进入赌场，特别是霍赫希尔德（Hochschild，1983）的“情绪劳动”和莱德纳（Leidner，1999）的“员工-顾客-管理层利益联盟”。尽管这些概念对于描述赌场工作人员的劳动过程动态大有裨益，但我发现它们不足以将劳动过程置于企业自身的结构及其组织环境的历史变迁之中。因此，我重新审视了制造业文献。具体而言，我借鉴了布拉沃伊（Burawoy，1998）的“霸权体制”概念，来描述杰克波特赌场劳动力控制方法的历史渊源（管理结构的解耦和赌场工作人员的技术再培训）、员工如何体验这种方法（将其视为“赚取小费的游戏”），以及这种方法对企业的更广泛作用（提供定制化服务和降低劳动力成本）。通过这种方式，我的研究能够重构当代服务经济中关于劳动过程的主流理论，从而实现广泛的普适性。此外，最后我将探讨这项研究的发现如何直接应用于更一般的服务人员类别：小费员工。

Blackjack dealing is an admittedly extreme occupation. Its practitioners work in an environment of potentially total surveillance, intense routinization of their labor, and no union protection. Precisely because of these factors, however, dealing offers a unique opportunity to refine the understanding of the labor process for tipped service workers. For if new service demands have here led to a hegemonic labor regime, then one should expect to find similar arrangements in less strictly regulated workplaces. In fact, since 1965, the number of U.S. employees who work for tips has tripled from approximately 1.9 million to 5.6 million (Jacobs, 2001; U.S. Department of Labor, 1965). Scholars of service work, however, have glossed over tipping, treating it as just one mechanism among many for inducing service provision from workers. This omission stems from a lack of attention to a second pressure generated by increased competition in service industries; while emotional labor demands arise, so do pressures to cut costs. And although the efficacy of new managerial strategies for inducing service from workers is well documented, one must not forget that their costs (in terms of training, supervision, etc.) are high. Tipping, insofar as it simultaneously lowers labor costs and spontaneously generates customized service provision, thus represents an attractive system for organizing work, albeit one with costs to the firm.
毋庸置疑，二十一点发牌员是一项极其特殊的职业。从业者身处一个可能受到全面监控、工作高度程式化且缺乏工会保护的环境中。然而，正因如此，发牌员为我们提供了一个独特的契机，让我们得以深入了解靠小费为生的服务人员的劳动过程。如果新的服务需求在这里催生了一种主导性的劳动制度，那么我们应该预期在监管不那么严格的工作场所也能发现类似的安排。事实上，自 1965 年以来，美国靠小费为生的雇员人数已经增长了两倍，从约 190 万增至 560 万（Jacobs，2001；美国劳工部，1965）。然而，服务业研究学者却往往忽略了小费，将其视为众多促使员工提供服务的机制之一。这种疏忽源于对服务业竞争加剧所带来的另一种压力的忽视：在情感劳动需求上升的同时，削减成本的压力也随之而来。尽管新的管理策略在提升员工服务水平方面的有效性已得到充分证实，但我们不能忽视这些策略的成本（包括培训、监督等）很高。小费制度既能降低劳动力成本，又能自发地促使员工提供个性化服务，因此，尽管它也会给企业带来成本，但它仍然是一种颇具吸引力的工作组织方式。

Indeed, in certain, typically highly competitive, sectors of the service economy (such as restaurant and tourism industries), the labor process for practically all workers (food servers, bartenders, valets, cab drivers, etc.) is organized around a tipping system. And although the specificity of each will vary, certain characteristics are to be found in all, leading to hegemonic labor regimes. First, outsourcing the task of paying workers to clients generates immediate benefits to the firm. Labor costs are lowered and service customized as workers develop and share among themselves a typology of customer types and service expectations. Yet this outsourcing constitutes as well a loss of control. Workers’ and management’s interests may at times coincide—both restaurant management and food servers, for example, have an interest in ensuring that food arrives at tables hot and fresh. When their interests diverge, though, workers will act to increase their tips by either aligning with clients (such as when dealers help players win) or acting independently (as when dealers antagonize nontippers). Servers thus provide patrons free food and drinks to encourage larger tips (Paules, 1991), and cocktail waitresses treat rudely female patrons and other notoriously bad tippers (Spradley & Mann, 1975).
事实上，在某些竞争异常激烈的服务经济领域（例如餐饮和旅游业），几乎所有劳动者（服务员、调酒师、代客泊车员、出租车司机等）的劳动过程都围绕着小费制度展开。尽管具体情况各有不同，但所有劳动者都具有一些共同特征，从而形成了主导性的劳动制度。首先，将支付员工工资的任务外包给客户，能为公司带来立竿见影的好处。随着员工逐渐形成并分享客户类型和服务期望的分类，劳动力成本得以降低，服务也更加个性化。然而，这种外包也意味着控制权的丧失。员工和管理层的利益有时可能一致——例如，餐厅管理层和服务员都希望确保食物新鲜热乎地送到餐桌上。但当他们的利益出现分歧时，员工会通过与客户结盟（例如荷官帮助玩家赢钱）或采取独立行动（例如荷官激怒不给小费的顾客）来增加小费收入。因此，服务员会向顾客提供免费的食物和饮料，以鼓励他们多给小费（Paules，1991），而鸡尾酒女服务员则会粗鲁地对待女性顾客和其他出了名吝啬的小费者（Spradley & Mann，1975）。

Yet management allows tipping systems to regulate the labor process only for certain categories of clients. They are generally appropriate for the organization’s “low-rollers” insofar as they encourage maximum speed and at least moderate service provision. Consider the restaurant industry, where tips are relatively standardized (a moderately competent server expects to receive around 15% from every party). Whereas management requires servers to provide courteous service to all diners, the primary worker strategy for maximizing tips (especially at the lunch hour or in low-end diners) is to quickly cycle diners in and out, a tactic that requires at times treating customers rudely (Paules, 1991). Management here accepts noncompliance with the organization’s display rules because it cares most about maximizing speed for such patrons anyway.
然而，管理层只允许对特定类型的顾客实行小费制度来规范劳动流程。这种制度通常适用于餐厅的“小额顾客”，因为它能鼓励服务员尽可能提高服务速度，并至少提供中等水平的服务。以餐饮业为例，小费相对标准化（一个中等水平的服务员期望从每桌顾客那里获得大约 15%的小费）。虽然管理层要求服务员为所有顾客提供礼貌周到的服务，但员工最大化小费的主要策略（尤其是在午餐时间或低档餐厅）是快速地让顾客进出餐厅，这种策略有时需要对顾客态度粗鲁（Paules，1991）。管理层之所以容忍员工不遵守公司规定的小费发放规则，是因为他们最关心的无论如何都要尽可能提高这类顾客的用餐速度。

In turn, for the organization’s “high-rollers,” the firm, to ensure maximum service, should tighten control. A restaurant may thus reserve for well-known clients or wealthy “regulars” a special section of the dining area in which surveillance is tightened or managers serve patrons themselves. Workers in turn should accept as legitimate heightened control, the necessary cost of autonomy otherwise. In conclusion, as the U.S. service economy continues to expand and become increasingly competitive, we would expect management, when possible, to allow tipping systems to regulate the labor process as a means of both lowering labor costs and inducing workers to perform service with a smile. The noncompliance that results will in turn be regulated in accordance with the financial or otherwise status of the “player,” thus constituting a hegemonic regime of service provision.
反过来，为了确保为机构中的“大客户”提供最佳服务，公司应该加强管控。例如，餐厅可能会为知名客户或富有的“常客”预留用餐区的特定区域，加强监控，甚至由经理亲自服务顾客。员工也应该接受这种合法的加强管控，并将其视为失去自主权的必要代价。总之，随着美国服务经济的持续扩张和竞争的日益激烈，我们预期管理层应尽可能允许小费制度来规范劳动过程，以此降低劳动力成本并激励员工面带微笑地提供服务。由此产生的违规行为将根据“参与者”的经济或其他身份进行相应的监管，从而形成一种服务供给的霸权机制。
